## Cotton Farming Guide for Maharashtra

### Introduction
Cotton (Gossypium spp.) is one of the most important cash crops in Maharashtra, India. The state is the second-largest producer of cotton in the country, contributing about 26% of total national production.

### Soil Requirements
- **Soil Type**: Black cotton soil (regur) is ideal, with deep, well-drained loamy soil
- **pH Level**: 6.0 to 8.0 (neutral to slightly alkaline)
- **Soil Moisture**: 30-40% during the growing season is optimal
- **Organic Matter**: Minimum 0.5% organic carbon content recommended

### Climate Requirements
- **Temperature**: 21-30°C optimal; can tolerate up to 40°C
- **Rainfall**: 600-1200mm annually
- **Growing Season**: Kharif (June-October)
- **Frost**: Cotton is sensitive to frost; avoid planting in frost-prone areas

### Irrigation Management
Cotton requires careful water management for optimal yields:

1. **Pre-sowing Irrigation**: 50-60mm to prepare the soil
2. **Vegetative Stage**: 40-50mm every 15 days
3. **Flowering Stage**: 60-70mm every 10 days (critical period)
4. **Boll Development**: 50-60mm every 12 days
5. **Maturity Stage**: Reduce irrigation to promote boll opening

**Total Water Requirement**: 700-1200mm over the crop cycle

### Common Pests
- Bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera)
- Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
- Pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella)
- Aphids (Aphis gossypii)

### Pest Management
- Use Integrated Pest Management (IPM) practices
- Install pheromone traps for bollworm monitoring
- Apply neem-based pesticides for early infestations
- Consider Bt cotton varieties for bollworm resistance

## Wheat Cultivation in Punjab

### Overview
Wheat is the staple crop of Punjab, with the state contributing about 17% of India's total wheat production. It is a Rabi season crop grown from October to April.

### Optimal Conditions
- **Soil**: Well-drained loamy soil
- **Soil Moisture**: 20-50% during different growth stages
- **Temperature**: 10-25°C (cool weather preferred)
- **Rainfall**: 40-100mm during growing season

### Irrigation Schedule
Wheat requires 4-6 irrigations during the crop cycle:

1. **Crown Root Initiation (21 DAS)**: Critical irrigation
2. **Tillering Stage (40-45 DAS)**: Important for tiller development
3. **Jointing Stage (60-65 DAS)**: Crucial for stem elongation
4. **Flowering Stage (80-85 DAS)**: Critical for grain setting
5. **Milk Stage (100-105 DAS)**: Important for grain filling
6. **Dough Stage (115-120 DAS)**: Final irrigation if needed

### Varieties for Punjab
- HD 3086 (High yielding)
- PBW 725 (Disease resistant)
- WH 1105 (Good quality grain)
- DBW 187 (Heat tolerant)

## Rice Cultivation in Tamil Nadu

### Paddy Farming Overview
Rice is the primary food crop in Tamil Nadu, with three main seasons: Kuruvai (June-September), Samba (August-January), and Navarai (February-May).

### Water Requirements
Rice is a water-intensive crop requiring standing water:

- **Land Preparation**: Saturate soil with 50-100mm water
- **Transplanting to Tillering**: Maintain 2-5cm standing water
- **Panicle Initiation**: 5-7cm water depth
- **Flowering to Grain Fill**: 5cm water depth
- **Maturity**: Drain field 10-15 days before harvest

**Total Water Requirement**: 1200-2000mm per crop

### Soil Moisture Monitoring
- Keep soil moisture above 60% during critical stages
- Allow Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) to save water
- Use tensiometers for precise irrigation scheduling

### Key Varieties
- ADT 43 (Short duration)
- CO 51 (High yielding)
- BPT 5204 (Fine grain quality)
- ADT 36 (Drought tolerant)

## Irrigation Best Practices for Indian Agriculture

### General Guidelines

1. **Monitor Soil Moisture**: Use soil moisture sensors at 15cm depth
2. **Time of Irrigation**: Early morning or late evening to reduce evaporation
3. **Irrigation Efficiency**: Drip irrigation (90% efficiency) vs. flood irrigation (50-60%)
4. **Water Quality**: EC should be less than 2 dS/m for most crops

### Sensor-Based Irrigation
Modern irrigation systems use soil moisture sensors to optimize water use:

- **Capacitive Sensors**: Measure volumetric water content
- **Tensiometers**: Measure soil water tension
- **TDR Probes**: Time Domain Reflectometry for accuracy
- **Placement**: Install sensors at 15cm (root zone) and 30cm (deep root zone)

### Decision Thresholds
- Below 20% soil moisture: Immediate irrigation needed
- 20-35%: Monitor closely, irrigate within 2-3 days
- 35-55%: Optimal range for most crops
- Above 55%: Reduce irrigation, ensure drainage

## Pest and Disease Management

### Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Principles

1. **Cultural Control**: Crop rotation, resistant varieties, proper spacing
2. **Biological Control**: Predators, parasites, pathogens
3. **Chemical Control**: Judicious use of pesticides as last resort
4. **Mechanical Control**: Traps, barriers, manual removal

### Common Diseases in Indian Agriculture

| Crop | Disease | Symptoms | Management |
|------|---------|----------|------------|
| Cotton | Bacterial blight | Angular leaf spots | Resistant varieties, copper sprays |
| Wheat | Rust | Orange-brown pustules | Fungicides, resistant varieties |
| Rice | Blast | Diamond-shaped lesions | Tricyclazole spray, resistant varieties |
| Tomato | Late blight | Water-soaked spots | Mancozeb spray, proper spacing |

## Fertilizer Recommendations

### Macro-Nutrients (NPK)

**Cotton (Maharashtra)**:
- Nitrogen: 60-80 kg/ha (split application)
- Phosphorus: 30-40 kg/ha (basal dose)
- Potassium: 30-40 kg/ha (basal dose)

**Wheat (Punjab)**:
- Nitrogen: 120-150 kg/ha (3 splits)
- Phosphorus: 60 kg/ha (basal)
- Potassium: 40 kg/ha (basal)

**Rice (Tamil Nadu)**:
- Nitrogen: 100-120 kg/ha (4 splits)
- Phosphorus: 50 kg/ha (basal)
- Potassium: 50 kg/ha (2 splits)

### Micro-Nutrients
- Zinc sulfate: 25 kg/ha for zinc-deficient soils
- Iron sulfate: 50 kg/ha for calcareous soils
- Boron: 0.5 kg/ha for oil crops

## Weather-Based Advisory

### Monsoon Season (June-September)
- Expect heavy rainfall; ensure proper drainage
- Monitor for fungal diseases in humid conditions
- Reduce fertilizer application before heavy rains

### Winter Season (October-February)
- Watch for frost in North India
- Optimize irrigation for Rabi crops
- Apply foliar sprays in morning when dew evaporates

### Summer Season (March-May)
- Increase irrigation frequency
- Use mulching to reduce evaporation
- Provide shade for sensitive crops
